# Domain boundary: what imagery can verify

## Suitable screening tasks

- Whether a curb ramp appears to be present at a visible corner.
- Whether the ramp is visibly obstructed or severely damaged.
- Whether a detectable-warning surface appears to be present.
- Whether imagery and an existing inventory appear to disagree.
- Which records should be prioritized for field review.

## Claims that require measurement or professional review

- Exact running slope and cross slope.
- Clear width and precise grade-break geometry.
- Detectable-warning dimensions, spacing, and contrast.
- Drainage performance and surface tolerances.
- Formal compliance with current accessibility standards.

The system is designed for inventory QA and review prioritization. It does not issue an
ADA or PROWAG compliance determination.

## Error costs

A false negative can hide a missing or damaged ramp and delay review. A false positive
can create an inaccurate inventory record or waste field-inspection time. The operating
threshold should reflect the workflow. A screening tool may favor recall, followed by
human review. An automatic inventory update requires much higher precision and stronger
evidence.
